Group versus private retreats: Choosing the right setting for your inner journey

Deciding to participate in a plant medicine or psychedelic retreat is a significant step toward personal growth, healing, and self-discovery. However, once you make the commitment to do this inner work, you face another crucial choice: should you join a group retreat or opt for a private setting for one or two participants?

Both formats offer profound opportunities for transformation, but they cater to very different psychological needs, personalities, and intentions. Understanding how the setting influences your experience is key to choosing the environment where you can surrender most fully and safely.

The dynamics of a group retreat: Shared resonance and community

For many, the prospect of undertaking a deep psychological journey alongside others can feel intimidating at first. Yet, group retreats remain one of the most traditional and transformative ways to experience plant medicine.

Humans are fundamentally social creatures, and much of our emotional conditioning, trauma, and behavioral patterns are formed in relation to others. Participating in a retreat with a small, curated group creates a unique container for collective healing.

The power of shared vulnerability

Watching others process deep emotions, confront personal challenges, and express vulnerability creates an immediate sense of shared humanity. It dissolves the common illusion that you are alone in your struggles. Hearing someone else articulate a feeling you have carried in silence for years can trigger profound breakthroughs even before your own session begins.

Mirroring and interpersonal insights

Group settings act as a microcosm of everyday life. The interactions you have with fellow participants, whether during group circles, shared meals, or quiet moments, often mirror your relational habits outside the retreat. You may notice patterns of social anxiety, a tendency to care for others at your own expense, or a fear of being judged. Recognizing these dynamics in real time offers valuable material for integration.

Collective energy and cost efficiency

There is a tangible synergy in a shared ceremonial space. Many participants report feeling supported by the collective focus and intention of the room. Additionally, group retreats make high-level professional facilitation, screening, and venue amenities more accessible from a financial perspective.

The dynamics of a private retreat: Absolute focus and deep personalization

While group settings excel at community and relational learning, private retreats provide an environment built entirely around your individual pace, history, and comfort level. In a private retreat, the setting and support are dedicated to you or to you and the person joining you.

Tailored pace and complete privacy

Private retreats can be particularly suitable for participants who value greater privacy, individualized attention, a quieter setting, or who feel less comfortable processing personal experiences in a group. In a private setting, you do not need to worry about how you look, sound, or react in front of others. You have total freedom to express grief, anger, or joy without the subtle fear of disturbing anyone else or being observed.

Individualized facilitation and depth

During a private retreat, the facilitators focus entirely on your physical and emotional cues. The preparation, session guidance, and integration are customized to your specific psychological background and goals. If a session requires extra time, quiet space, or targeted somatic support, the team can adjust immediately to serve your needs.

Ideal for high-profile individuals and intense processing

For public figures, executives, or leaders who spend their daily lives held to high expectations, setting down the "public persona" in a group can be difficult. A private retreat offers a completely confidential sanctuary where control can be surrendered safely.

Comparing the settings: Key factors to consider

To help you decide which environment aligns best with your current situation, consider how these two settings compare across essential aspects of the experience:

Aspect Group Retreat Private Retreat
Primary focus Shared healing, community connection, and relational insights Deep individual processing, total privacy, and personal customization
Social dynamic Interactive; includes group sharing circles and collective space Exclusive; interaction is limited to you and your facilitation team
Pace and schedule Structured around the collective group rhythm Fully adaptable to your personal energy, timing, and needs
Best suited for Those seeking connection, shared validation, and relational growth Those who prefer privacy, individualized attention and settings more quiet

How to choose the right path for your journey

There is no universally superior option; the right choice depends on where you are in your life right now. Ask yourself the following questions:

  • What is your primary intention? If you are looking to heal feelings of isolation, practice vulnerability, or build a sense of connection, a group retreat is often the most impactful choice. If your goal is to process a specific, deep-seated trauma or work through complex personal questions without distraction, a private setting may be more effective.
  • How does your nervous system respond to groups? If being around unfamiliar people makes you feel safe and supported, a group will enrich your experience. If group environments trigger hypervigilance, performance anxiety, or a need to mask your feelings, a private setting will allow your nervous system to relax more easily.
  • What is your history with inner work? Beginners who feel nervous about the unknown often find comfort in the shared energy of a group. Conversely, experienced journeyers or individuals carrying heavy emotional burdens sometimes prefer the undivided attention of a dedicated facilitator.
